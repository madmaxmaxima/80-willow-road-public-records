## I. Key Legal Facts for Congressional Referral

1. **Willow Project LLC** is a Delaware LLC controlled by **Vitaly Yusufov**, a Russian citizen whose father **Igor Yusufov** served as Russia's Energy Minister (2001-2004) and sat on the boards of Gazprom (2003-2013) and Rosneft.

2. The ~$72M purchase of the property in 2018 was **never filed with CFIUS** for voluntary review.

3. **Deutsche Bank** initially rejected the transaction after its New York compliance office flagged reputational concerns. A European committee overruled the rejection. A Deutsche Bank employee subsequently filed a **Suspicious Activity Report (SAR)** with FinCEN.

4. Vitaly Yusufov is on **Ukraine's sanctions list** (NSDC decision dated February 2, 2021, enacted by presidential decree No. 43/2021).

5. **Moffett Federal Airfield** (NASA Ames Research Center, home of the California Air National Guard 129th Rescue Wing, Army DEVCOM AvMC Design/Simulation/Experimentation Group for rotorcraft technologies — Army.mil Jan 11 2022 — and 7th Psychological Operations Group) is approximately **5 miles** from the property (consistent measurement used across repository; prior 12-mile figure corrected to 5 miles per mapping verification).

6. **CFIUS jurisdictional analysis:**
 - **Part 802 (real estate):** Moffett Field is **NOT on the CFIUS Appendix A list**. Even if it were, the **urbanized area exception** (31 C.F.R. § 802.211) would likely preclude jurisdiction since Menlo Park is within the SF-Oakland urbanized area (Census Bureau).
 - **Part 800 (covered transaction):** STRONGER path. Willow Project LLC is a U.S. business. Vitaly Yusufov (Russian citizen) controls it. The 2018 acquisition constitutes a "covered transaction" under 31 C.F.R. § 800.301, with **no urbanized area exception** applicable.
 - **Non-notified transaction review:** CFIUS can review transactions that were never voluntarily notified indefinitely (Congressional Research Service IF10177: "Non-notified transactions remain subject indefinitely to future CFIUS review and possible divestment or other actions mandated by the President"). Authority: 31 C.F.R. § 800.501, 50 U.S.C. § 4565, CRS IF10177 (Aug 2026). Treasury's Nov 2024 final rule expanded CFIUS authority to compel information from third parties (banks, underwriters, service providers) to assess non-notified transactions (Treasury JY2716). New transactions (development, financing, entity restructuring) could additionally constitute new covered transactions.

7. **Corporate Transparency Act (DEFANGED):** The CTA was supposed to require Willow Project LLC to file Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) reports with FinCEN. However, FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule exempted all domestic entities and U.S. persons from CTA reporting. Willow Project LLC, as a Delaware LLC, is now exempt. The veil that the CTA was designed to pierce remains intact. FinCEN's separate Residential Real Estate Reporting Rule (effective March 1, 2026) applies only to future transactions.

## IV. Template Congressional Referral Letter

### Version A: To Local Representative (Liccardo)

---

The Honorable Sam Liccardo
United States House of Representatives
Washington, DC 20515

RE: Request for CFIUS Referral and Congressional Inquiry Regarding Foreign-Controlled Real Estate Acquisition at 80 Willow Road, Menlo Park, California

Dear Representative Liccardo:

I write as a constituent of California's 16th Congressional District to request your assistance in referring a matter to the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS) and to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) for review.

**The Transaction**

In 2018, Willow Project LLC, a Delaware limited liability company, acquired the former Sunset Magazine headquarters at 80 Willow Road, Menlo Park, for approximately $72 million. Willow Project LLC is controlled by Vitaly Yusufov, a Russian national whose father, Igor Yusufov, served as Russia's Minister of Energy from 2001 to 2004 and held board positions at Gazprom (2003-2013) and Rosneft, two of Russia's largest state-connected energy companies.

This transaction was never filed for voluntary review by CFIUS. However, several facts raise serious national security questions:

1. **Deutsche Bank Compliance Flag:** Deutsche Bank's New York compliance office initially rejected the transaction due to reputational concerns associated with the Yusufov family's ties to the Russian government. The rejection was overruled by a European committee within the bank. A Deutsche Bank employee subsequently filed a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) with the U.S. Treasury's FinCEN.

2. **Ukrainian Sanctions:** Vitaly Yusufov is listed on Ukraine's sanctions registry pursuant to a decision by the National Security and Defense Council of Ukraine dated February 2, 2021 (enacted by Presidential Decree No. 43/2021).

3. **Proximity to Military Installations:** The property is approximately 5 miles from Moffett Federal Airfield, which houses NASA Ames Research Center and the California Air National Guard 129th Rescue Wing. While Moffett Field is not currently listed in CFIUS Appendix A, the proximity of a foreign-controlled property to sensitive federal facilities is relevant under the broader Part 800 covered transaction framework.

4. **State Enforcement Pressure:** On July 29, 2026, California Attorney General Rob Bonta issued an AB 712 notice to the City of Menlo Park threatening up to $6.65 million in mandatory fines ($10,000 per unit for 665 proposed units) if the city does not approve the development. The effect of this state enforcement action is to pressure local government approval of a massive development that directly benefits the financial interests of a foreign person with documented connections to the Russian government.

**Request for Action**

I respectfully request:

1. **CFIUS Referral:** That your office refer this matter to CFIUS for review of the 2018 acquisition as a non-notified covered transaction under 31 C.F.R. § 800.501. Under Part 800, the acquisition of control of a U.S. business (Willow Project LLC) by a foreign person (Vitaly Yusufov) constitutes a covered transaction regardless of the property's location relative to military installations. The urbanized area exception in Part 802 does not apply to Part 800 covered transactions.

2. **FinCEN SAR Follow-Up Inquiry:** That your office inquire with FinCEN regarding the status of the suspicious activity report (SAR) filed by Deutsche Bank after the 2018 closing. Given the Yusufov family's documented ties to the Russian government, Ukrainian sanctions status, and the complex LLC/BVI shell company structure, it is critical to know whether this SAR generated any follow-up investigation. Note that FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule exempted domestic entities like Willow Project LLC from the Corporate Transparency Act's beneficial ownership reporting requirements, meaning the veil of LLC opacity that the CTA was designed to pierce remains intact for this transaction.

3. **Congressional Inquiry to CFIUS:** That your office send a letter to the CFIUS chair (Secretary of the Treasury) asking (a) whether CFIUS has ever reviewed or was aware of this transaction, (b) whether the transaction was identified through CFIUS's non-notified transaction monitoring process as required by FIRRMA, and (c) why, if it was not identified, a transaction involving a family with documented ties to the Russian government that prompted a Deutsche Bank SAR was not flagged.

I note that your office has recently demonstrated leadership on CFIUS matters, including your January 2026 call for CFIUS review of the Paramount-WBD acquisition. The 80 Willow Road situation presents analogous concerns: a transaction involving significant foreign interests that has not received the regulatory scrutiny mandated by Congress when it enacted FIRRMA.

I am available to provide additional documentation, including corporate filings, ownership chain analysis, sanctions registry entries, and copies of Public Records Act requests filed with the California Attorney General and Department of Housing and Community Development.

## V-A. Legislative Status Tracker (Updated August 13, 2026)

### S.197 (Protecting Military Installations and Ranges Act of 2025)
- **Sponsor:** Cruz (R-TX), with Britt, Budd, Mullin, Scott (FL), Tuberville, Cotton
- **Introduced:** January 22, 2025
- **Referred to:** Senate Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs Committee
- **Current status:** INTRODUCED. No committee hearing scheduled. Last cosponsor additions: February 26 and March 4, 2025.
- **Bill text confirms:** Would require mandatory CFIUS review of real estate purchases within 100 miles of military installations by entities linked to Russia, China, Iran, or North Korea. Text uses "proposed, pending, or completed" language, meaning it would apply retroactively to the 2018 80 Willow acquisition.
- **Congressional Record:** Volume 171 (2025), S302 [22JA], S1397 [26FE], S1497 [4MR].

### Trump "America First Investment Policy" NSPM (February 2025)
- The National Security Presidential Memorandum proposes eliminating the urbanized area exception for foreign adversary real estate investments. Key language: the Administration "will use all necessary legal instruments, including CFIUS, to restrict PRC-affiliated persons from investing in United States technology, critical infrastructure, healthcare, agriculture, energy, raw materials, or other strategic sectors." The NSPM explicitly proposes "expansions in CFIUS authority to target Chinese greenfield investments with respect to real estate, including in urban areas."
- **Significance for 80 Willow:** If the urbanized area exception is eliminated through rulemaking (NPRM not yet published as of August 2026), Moffett Field's 12-mile proximity to 80 Willow would fall within Part 802's real estate jurisdiction directly, closing the gap that currently forces reliance on Part 800's covered transaction path.
- **Status:** NPRM not yet published. Treasury is reportedly developing implementing regulations. Congressional referral letters should reference the NSPM as evidence that the Administration itself recognizes the urbanized area exception as a gap.

### MineOne CFIUS Divestment Precedent (May 2024)
- President Biden ordered MineOne Partners (Chinese-owned crypto mining facility within 1 mile of F.E. Warren AFB, Wyoming) to divest after CFIUS investigation initiated by a public tip. Divestment ordered approximately 2 years post-purchase.
- **Relevance:** Establishes that CFIUS can and will order divestment of real estate near military installations based on tips from the public. Congressional referral letters should cite MineOne as the most recent divestment precedent. Key differences: 80 Willow is 5 miles (not 1 mile) from Moffett Field, and the acquisition is 8+ years old (not 2).

### November 2024 CFIUS Appendix A Expansion (89 Fed. Reg. 88128)
- Added 59 military installations to Part 802 coverage. California additions: Camp Roberts (Part 1), Air Force Plant 42 (Part 2).
- **Moffett Field remains NOT on Appendix A.** This means even the expanded real estate jurisdiction does not reach 80 Willow under Part 802. The Part 800 covered transaction path remains the strongest jurisdictional argument.

### FY2026 NDAA Section 8102 (P.L. 119-60, signed December 18, 2025) — NEW
- **Enacted into law.** The FY2026 NDAA (passed Senate 77-20 Oct 9, signed Dec 18) includes Section 8102, which expands CFIUS's process for identifying covered real estate by authorizing a broader list of **"national security-sensitive sites"** that may trigger jurisdiction. This explicitly includes **intelligence and energy installations** (such as national laboratories), not just military installations.
- Requires each CFIUS member agency to **annually review and update** sensitive site designations, with documented risk assessments reported to Congress.
- Allows CFIUS to **adjust the distance thresholds** for what constitutes "proximity."
- **Significance for 80 Willow:** NASA Ames Research Center at Moffett Field could be designated as a national security-sensitive site under Section 8102 given: (1) Army Aviation Development Directorate (DEVCOM AvMC) military research presence, (2) ITAR-controlled technology subject to prior Congressional investigation, (3) CA Air National Guard 129th Rescue Wing operations, (4) Pleiades supercomputer. If designated, and distance thresholds adjusted, 80 Willow Road (~5 miles away) could fall under Part 802 jurisdiction directly.
- **Status:** CFIUS member agency reviews underway per statutory mandate. No public rulemaking yet on updated site designations.
- The FIGHT China Act of 2025 (S.1053, Cornyn/bipartisan) was also incorporated into the NDAA, adding Russia/China agricultural land to CFIUS scope.
- Cruz's standalone S.197 remains in committee but some CFIUS expansion has been enacted through Section 8102. Congressional referral letters should cite Section 8102 as the legal hook for requesting Moffett Field designation.
