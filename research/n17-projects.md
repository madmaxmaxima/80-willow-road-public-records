## TCC-to-N17 Pipeline Analysis (Aug 26, 2026)

**Research thread #7: Heneghan's Trammell Crow projects and any connection to Yusufov family**

### Findings

1. **No direct TCC-Allrise/Yusufov connection found:** Extensive search of public records, CBRE/TCC press releases, real estate publications, and property records reveals no direct business relationship between Trammell Crow Company (or its parent CBRE) and Allrise Capital, Ruslan Zinurov, the Yusufov family, or Embarcadero Capital Partners. TCC's Bay Area projects (Cochrane Technology Center in Morgan Hill, 200 Twin Dolphin in Redwood Shores, Tracy 205 Logistics, Diridon Station in San Jose, One Meadow Glen in Millbrae, Alameda Point) are all with institutional partners (CBRE IM, Multi-Employer Property Trust, Bentall Kennedy).

2. **Embarcadero Capital Partners was intermediate seller:** Embarcadero Capital Partners (Belmont-based) bought 80 Willow from Time Inc. in 2014 for ~$75M. Deutsche Bank was brought in as co-owner on behalf of clients in 2016. Yusufov's Willow Project LLC bought from both in 2018 for $72M. No evidence that Heneghan or TCC had any relationship with Embarcadero Capital Partners or were involved in these transactions.

3. **Timeline indicates pre-arrangement:** Heneghan left TCC in June 2023 and had N17 registered and the 80 Willow preliminary application ready by July 2023 (filing two SB 330 permits simultaneously). This 30-day turnaround from company founding to filing the largest Builder's Remedy project in Bay Area history is incompatible with the relationship forming after departure. The Allrise Capital relationship must have predated N17's founding.

4. **Exclusive client concentration:** After founding N17, Heneghan filed for three additional Allrise Capital properties (598 Bryant, 650 Harrison, 875 Sansome) in January-February 2024, followed by a single non-Allrise project (Pleasanton with Black Mountain Properties in August 2025). The concentration is notable: 4 of 5 known N17 projects serve the Allrise/Yusufov network.

5. **Skills match but no TCC overlap:** Heneghan's TCC experience (offices, life science, multi-family) provides exactly the expertise needed for Builder's Remedy mega-projects. However, TCC's Northern California office projects are institutional JVs with mainstream partners. The departure appears to have been Heneghan leveraging his entitlement expertise for a specific client group, not a TCC-sanctioned spin-off.

6. **California Sun Podcast (Oct 2024):** Heneghan described N17 as "leading the Bay Area in pending planning applications for new housing" and emphasized the importance of "new laws for faster approvals." This framing positions Builder's Remedy exploitation as the core business model, consistent with the company being purpose-built for the Allrise/Yusufov strategy.

### Unresolved

- How Heneghan connected with the Allrise/Yusufov network remains unknown. Possible vectors: (a) TCC Bay Area network introduced him to Allrise's need for a developer, (b) Allen Matkins (N17's law firm) introduced the parties, (c) Heneghan identified the Builder's Remedy opportunity and approached property owners directly.
- Whether Heneghan has any equity interest in the projects or is operating purely as a developer-for-hire is not publicly documented.
- BDE Architecture (shared architect for 598 Bryant, 650 Harrison, 875 Sansome) may be a conduit worth investigating.
