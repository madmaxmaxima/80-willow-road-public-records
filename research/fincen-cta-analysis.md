## Willow Project LLC Beneficial Ownership Reporting

### Corporate Transparency Act Requirements

The Corporate Transparency Act (CTA), effective January 1, 2024, requires most U.S. entities (LLCs, corporations, etc.) to file Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) reports with FinCEN. Key requirements:

1. **Reporting Companies:** Any LLC or corporation created in or registered to do business in the United States, unless exempt (large companies with 20+ employees and $5M+ revenue, regulated entities, etc.).

2. **What must be reported:** Full legal name, date of birth, address, and ID document (passport/DL) for every individual who:
 - Exercises "substantial control" over the entity, OR
 - Owns or controls at least 25% of ownership interests

3. **Filing deadlines:**
 - Entities created before Jan 1, 2024: Must file by Jan 1, 2025
 - Entities created in 2024: 90 days from creation
 - Entities created 2025+: 30 days from creation

4. **Database access:** The FinCEN BOI database is NOT publicly searchable. Access is limited to:
 - Federal law enforcement and national security agencies
 - State/local law enforcement with a court order
 - Financial institutions with customer consent
 - Foreign law enforcement through intermediary federal agencies

### Applicability to Willow Project LLC

**Willow Project LLC** is a Delaware LLC. It was likely created before 2024 (the $72M property purchase was in 2018), meaning its BOI filing deadline was January 1, 2025.

**Key questions:**

1. **Has Willow Project LLC filed its BOI report?** If not, it is in violation of the CTA, subject to civil penalties of $591/day and criminal penalties up to $10,000 and 2 years imprisonment.

2. **Who is listed as the beneficial owner?** If Vitaly Yusufov is listed, FinCEN has a direct record linking a Ukrainian-sanctioned individual to the entity. If someone else is listed (e.g., Garrett Camporine of Canopic Advisory), that could constitute a false filing.

3. **Are related entities compliant?** Willow Park LLC, N17 Development, Allrise Capital Inc., EVN Investments LLC, Arc CW LLC, 2534 Benedict Canyon Dr LLC, Canopic Advisory, and Allrise Direct Lending LLC all appear to be reporting companies under the CTA.

### CTA Status: Domestic Entities EXEMPTED (March 2025)

**CRITICAL UPDATE (Aug 7, 2026):** On March 21, 2025, FinCEN issued an interim final rule that **exempts ALL domestic entities and U.S. persons from CTA BOI reporting requirements.** Only foreign reporting companies (entities formed under foreign law and registered to do business in the U.S.) must still file.

This means:
- **Willow Project LLC (Delaware LLC) is now EXEMPT from BOI reporting.** No filing obligation, no penalties for non-filing.
- The entire CTA compliance angle (noncompliance penalties, false filing exposure) is no longer viable as an investigative tool for domestic LLCs.
- The FinCEN BOI database will not contain beneficial ownership information for Willow Project LLC or any of the related domestic entities (Allrise Capital, EVN Investments, Canopic Advisory, etc.).

**Rationale cited by FinCEN/Treasury (March 2, 2025):** Reporting by domestic entities "would not serve the public interest" and "would not be highly useful in national security, intelligence, and law enforcement agency efforts."

**11th Circuit (Dec 17, 2025):** Upheld CTA as constitutional, but this is now moot for domestic entities given the executive exemption.

## FinCEN Residential Real Estate Reporting Rule (Effective March 1, 2026) — VACATED March 19, 2026

**NEW (Aug 7, 2026):** While the CTA was gutted for domestic entities, FinCEN's separate Residential Real Estate Reporting Rule took effect March 1, 2026. This rule requires reporting for certain non-financed transfers of residential real property to legal entities and trusts, including disclosure of beneficial ownership of transferee entities.

**Key provisions:**
- Applies to non-financed transfers of residential real property (1-4 family structures, vacant land intended for residential use) to entities or trusts
- No minimum transaction value threshold
- Requires disclosure of transferee's beneficial owners, transaction price, and other details
- Reporting persons (settlement agents, title companies, attorneys) must file within 30-60 days of closing
- Records must be retained for 5 years

## FinCEN Final Rule Issued — CTA Permanently Gutted (Aug 11, 2026)

**Source:** U.S. Treasury press release (Aug 11, 2026); ABA Banking Journal (Aug 11, 2026); JDSupra/Venable LLP analysis (Aug 2026); Wikipedia CTA reporting requirements article.

**On August 11, 2026, FinCEN issued a final rule permanently removing BOI reporting requirements for U.S. companies and U.S. persons under the CTA. The rule became effective August 14, 2026.**

Key provisions of the final rule:
- Permanently exempts all U.S. companies from CTA beneficial ownership reporting (formalizing March 2025 interim rule)
- Exempts U.S. persons with FinCEN IDs from any obligation to update or correct previously provided information
- Eliminates requirement for foreign companies to report U.S. person "company applicants"
- Exempts foreign pooled investment vehicles registered in the U.S. from reporting U.S. person beneficial ownership
- **FinCEN will DELETE previously reported information by U.S. persons from the BOI database**
- Foreign entities must still report BOI for foreign individuals only

**Data deletion:** FinCEN confirmed it will purge information about individuals it "reasonably believes are U.S. persons" from the database, identified by U.S. passport, driver's license, or other indicators. This means any BOI data Willow Project LLC or related entities may have previously filed is being destroyed.

**Supreme Court:** A petition for certiorari on CTA constitutionality is still pending, but the case may become moot given the final rule effectively nullifies domestic reporting.

**State-level regimes:** The final rule does not affect state-level beneficial ownership disclosure regimes (e.g., New York LLC Transparency Act). California does not currently have an equivalent state-level BOI reporting requirement, so no alternative disclosure mechanism exists for Willow Project LLC (a Delaware LLC operating in California). California's attempt to create one (SB 1201, Sen. Durazo) passed the Senate 23-10 in May 2024 but died in the Assembly Banking and Finance Committee (failed deadline 6/3/24). No successor bill has been introduced in the 2025-2026 legislative session. The bill would have required public disclosure of beneficial owners on the Secretary of State website starting Jan 1, 2026, going further than the federal CTA by making the data publicly available (not just accessible to law enforcement). Its failure leaves California with zero state-level BOI transparency for LLCs. Source: CalChamber Final Status Report (Oct 2024).

Secretary Bessent: "President Trump promised to cut red tape, and this final rule delivers. Treasury is eliminating a burdensome reporting requirement for millions of law-abiding business owners without compromising our national security."

**Impact on 80 Willow:** The CTA is now permanently closed as an investigative tool for piercing the Willow Project LLC / Allrise Capital / EVN Investments ownership structure. The entities are all Delaware LLCs or corporations (domestic entities), and Vitaly Yusufov appears to hold U.S. residency (Hillsborough, CA address in SoS filings). Any previously filed BOI data is being actively deleted. The only remaining federal transparency mechanisms are:
1. CFIUS retroactive review (requires congressional referral or executive action)
2. OFAC designation (requires Treasury action)
3. FinCEN SAR investigation (Deutsche Bank's 2018 SAR exists independently of CTA)
4. Congressional investigation/subpoena power

This deepens the policy narrative: the transparency tool Congress specifically designed to expose beneficial ownership behind LLCs like Willow Project LLC was not merely gutted, it was dismantled and its collected data destroyed. The regulatory framework that allowed the Yusufov family to purchase $72M in California real estate through opaque LLC structures has now been reinforced by the same administration.

### CDD Rule Remains (Updated Aug 25, 2026)

Per Sidley Austin analysis of the final rule, FinCEN's 2016 Customer Due Diligence Rule (CDD Rule) remains in effect and is unaffected. The CDD Rule requires covered financial institutions to identify and verify beneficial owners of legal entity customers at account opening. With domestic companies no longer filing BOI reports with FinCEN, the CDD Rule is now the **primary source of domestic beneficial ownership information** available to the financial system and law enforcement. FinCEN has stated it intends to modify the CDD Rule but has not committed to a timeline or indicated direction. This means:

- Deutsche Bank's initial beneficial ownership review of the Yusufov purchase (which led to flagging Vitaly as "politically significant person" and initially rejecting the $72M transaction) occurred under CDD obligations
- Any future bank transactions involving Willow Project LLC entities would still trigger CDD beneficial ownership identification
- But CDD data stays with the bank, not with FinCEN, unless a SAR is filed

### State-Level Transparency Landscape (Updated Aug 25, 2026)

**New York is the ONLY U.S. state with a CTA-analogue law in effect** (NY LLC Transparency Act, effective Jan 1, 2026). Source: ABA Business Law Today (Dec 2025), confirming California (SB 738/1201), Maryland (SB 954), and Massachusetts (H. 3566) all considered and failed to enact similar measures.

After FinCEN's March 2025 narrowing (which the Aug 11 final rule made permanent), the NYLTA's scope has been narrowed to match: per Gibson Dunn (Jan 2026), the Act now only requires non-U.S. entities registered in New York to report BOI for their non-U.S. beneficial owners. U.S. entities and U.S. beneficial owners are exempt.
