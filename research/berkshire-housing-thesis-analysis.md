# Following Berkshire Into Housing: A $100M Deployment Analysis

*Prepared August 9, 2026 — primary-source verification of a third-party investment thesis*

---

## Executive Summary

The thesis's analysis is sharp. Most of his claims check out against primary sources, with a few corrections on specifics. The core thesis — that the American housing supply chain is being consolidated by patient capital at cyclically depressed valuations, and that the structural supply deficit makes this a generational entry point — is sound. But deploying $100M into this thesis requires precision on *how*, not just *whether*.

**Verdict: Yes, follow the thesis. But not by chasing Berkshire's exact trades.** The $100M should be split across public equity (remaining acquisition targets trading below book), a direct BTR platform (the ROAD Act just handed institutional builders a permanent exemption), and supply-chain companies (the picks-and-shovels play Berkshire already owns).

## 4. 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act — ✅ Verified as Law

### Key Facts (All Confirmed)

| Claim | Status | Source |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Became law July 11, 2026 | ✅ | Wikipedia, Mondaq, Mayer Brown, KBRA, BPC |
| Without Trump's signature | ✅ "automatically became law after no action was taken by President Trump within the required timeframe" | Mondaq |
| 350-home institutional investor threshold | ✅ "entities controlling at least 350 single-family homes" | Wikipedia, KBRA, Mondaq |
| Build-to-rent got permanent exemption | ✅ "build-to-rent projects are now exempt as a qualifying excluded purchase" | Mondaq |
| Senate version had 7-year forced sale | ✅ "the previously proposed seven-year divestment requirement has been eliminated" | Mondaq, Mayer Brown |
| House stripped it | ✅ "The final text materially changes the Senate-passed homeownership provisions by removing the seven-year disposition framework" | Mayer Brown |
| Senate passed 85-5 | ✅ | Chicago Agent Magazine |
| House passed 358-32 | ✅ | Chicago Agent Magazine |
| Effective 180 days after enactment | ✅ (~January 8, 2027) | Mondaq |
| 15-year sunset | ✅ | Mondaq |

### What This Means for the Thesis

This is the most important piece of the puzzle. The ROAD Act creates a **two-tier housing market:**

1. **Existing home purchases:** Banned for institutions with 350+ SFR units. Invitation Homes, AMH, and other SFR REITs cannot grow through acquisition.
2. **Build-to-rent:** Permanently exempted. Purpose-built rental communities are explicitly carved out.
3. **Newly constructed homes:** Also exempted ("qualifying excluded purchase" for newly constructed units).

**Implication:** The ROAD Act is a *massive* moat for homebuilders with BTR platforms. Taylor Morrison's Yardly, D.R. Horton's BTR arm, and Lennar's Quarterra become even more valuable as the only legal institutional on-ramp to single-family rental housing.

The thesis is right that this is the single most important structural change. The Senate wanted to kill build-to-rent. The House saved it. Berkshire bought Taylor Morrison (with its Yardly BTR arm) knowing this was coming.

### Recommended $100M Deployment Strategy

#### Tier 1: Public Equity — Homebuilder Acquisition Targets ($35-40M)

The rule of thumb from UBS analyst John Lovallo: "Companies that have significant discounts to book have been looked at." Every recent acquisition has occurred at 0.8-1.0x book.

**Priority positions:**
- **Century Communities (CCS) at 0.6x book:** Most undervalued on the list. 16-state footprint in affordable housing — exactly the segment with the deepest structural demand. $1.5B market cap means a $100M position gives meaningful exposure.
- **LGI Homes (LGIH) at 0.5x book:** Deepest discount. Spec builder with strong margins from on-balance-sheet land strategy. At 0.5x book, the market is pricing in permanent impairment that the housing shortage contradicts.
- **KB Home (KBH) at 0.8x book:** Larger ($3.1B) and more liquid. Built-to-order model means lower inventory risk. Relocating HQ to Tempe (Sun Belt bet). If Berkshire or a Japanese buyer comes knocking, this is at the top of the list.
- **Meritage (MTH) at 0.9x book:** Largest remaining midsize target. Energy-efficient spec builder. Arizona HQ, Sun Belt focus. $4.4B market cap means it's right in the Berkshire/Sumitomo strike zone.

**Allocation:** $8-10M in each of CCS, LGIH, KBH, MTH. These are not just "wait for buyout" trades — at 0.5-0.9x book in a market with a 4.7M-unit housing deficit, the reversion-to-mean upside is substantial.

#### Tier 2: Build-to-Rent Platform ($25-30M)

The ROAD Act just created the most asymmetric opportunity in housing. Build-to-rent is now the **only** legal path for institutional investors with 350+ homes to grow in single-family. The 7-year forced sale is gone. The exemption is permanent. Existing SFR REITs (INVH, AMH) can't acquire existing homes — they must build or buy from builders.

**Options at $100M scale:**
- **Joint venture with a regional BTR developer** (NexMetro, Christopher Todd Capital, or similar). $25-30M buys a meaningful LP stake in a Sun Belt BTR platform. Target: 500-1,000 purpose-built homes across 3-5 communities.
- **Direct BTR development** in undersupplied markets. Phoenix, Dallas-Fort Worth, Columbus, Kansas City — the top BTR markets per CBRE. Land acquisition + horizontal development, then either hold for rental yield or sell communities to institutional buyers (who now can't acquire existing homes).
- **Key metric:** BTR vacancy rates were 6.9% nationally (CBRE Q2 2024); rent growth positive despite broader softness.

#### Tier 3: Housing Supply Chain ($20-25M)

Berkshire already owns the supply chain. You can't buy Clayton or Shaw. But you can buy their public competitors:

- **Building materials:** Summit Materials (SUM), Eagle Materials (EXP), AZEK Company (AZEK), Installed Building Products (IBP)
- **Manufactured/modular housing:** Cavco Industries (CVCO), Skyline Champion (SKY) — the ROAD Act eliminates the chassis requirement for manufactured housing, which is a massive regulatory unlock
- **Mortgage/financial services:** UWM Holdings (UWMC), Mr. Cooper (COOP) — rate-sensitive but positioned for a volume recovery when rates eventually normalize

#### Tier 4: Land Banking ($10-15M)

Patient capital's ultimate advantage. Homebuilder land banks are 5-7 year assets. At current valuations, entitled lots in Sun Belt growth corridors are available below replacement cost because builders are reducing their land investment (Taylor Morrison cut land investment from $593M to ~$533M in Q3 2025).

**Target:** Entitled lots in Texas, Florida, Arizona, Carolinas. Partner with a land-banking operator (Walton, Forestar/D.R. Horton, or a private fund). This is the Berkshire playbook — buy assets nobody else can afford to hold.

## 8. Counterarguments & Risks

### Housing Shortage Estimates — Which Is Credible?

| Source | Estimate | Methodology | Credibility |
|--------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Zillow** | **4.74M** (July 2026) | Counts families living in other families' units vs. vacant units for sale/rent | ✅ Most recent, held steady YoY for first time. Transparent methodology. **Best current estimate.** |
| **Up For Growth** | 3.9M (2023) | Compares "headship rates" by age cohort (2000 vs 2021) to estimate pent-up demand | ✅ Credible but partially stale |
| **Freddie Mac** | 3.8M (2020) | Vacancy rate gap analysis (actual vs 13% target for "well-functioning market") + 400K young-adult pent-up demand | ✅ Established but stale (2020 data) |
| **NAHB** | 1.5M (2021) | Only counts low-vacancy metro areas; 800K rental + 750K for-sale to restore "long-run equilibrium" | ⚠️ Lowest estimate. Excludes seasonal/vacation vacancies and doesn't count structural underbuilding nationally. NAHB has industry incentive to undercount (lower shortage = less policy urgency on affordability mandates). |
| **National Low Income Housing Coalition** | 7.3M | Includes affordable housing gap for very-low-income renters | ⚠️ Highest estimate. Counts different thing — housing affordable to the lowest quintile. |
| **the thesis cited UBS at 7M** | ⚠️ Could not verify a UBS 7M figure in my searches | May reference a UBS research note not publicly available | |

**Bottom line:** The real shortage is probably 4-5M units. The range (1.5M to 7.3M) mostly reflects different definitions of "shortage." At current building rates (~1.3-1.4M starts/year) and household formation (~1.5M/year), the deficit is growing, not shrinking.

### Construction Labor Competition

- Construction added 22,000 jobs in July despite overall payrolls declining 23,000 — construction is poaching workers from other sectors
- Construction wages rising 3.8-3.9% YoY (BLS ECI) — faster than all-worker average of 3.1%
- **AI/data center capex is a real competitor.** Alphabet just raised $80-85B (Berkshire bought $10B of that equity). Meta spending $130-145B on capex. These data centers need the same electricians, concrete crews, and HVAC installers as housing.
- IIJA/IRA infrastructure spending adds another $350B+ of construction demand over the decade
- **This is the strongest counterargument.** Labor is the binding constraint on housing supply. Even with the ROAD Act's supply-side reforms, you can't build houses without workers — and the workers are being bid away by data centers paying 30-50% premiums.

### Interest Rate Trajectory Under Warsh

This is the wildcard:
- **Fed at 3.50-3.75%** (held steady June + July 2026)
- **Warsh is hawkish:** "We will deliver price stability" — 9 of 19 FOMC members now expect a rate hike by year-end
- **BofA forecasts:** Three 25bp hikes (Sep, Oct, Dec 2026) — most aggressive on Wall Street
- **Deutsche Bank:** Two 25bp hikes (Sep + Dec)
- **JPM Wealth Management:** Still expects hold through end of 2026
- **Post-July jobs report:** Rate hike odds for September fell to 43.9% (from 57% before) — the weak jobs data is the strongest argument against tightening

**Housing impact:** Mortgage rates at 6.5-6.7% are already the primary sales constraint. If Warsh hikes to 4.00-4.25%, mortgage rates could push to 7%+. This would deepen the sales freeze but *increase* the importance of the structural supply deficit (existing homeowners with 3% mortgages are even more locked in). Paradoxically, higher rates make the *eventual* thaw more powerful for builders with land position and BTR platforms.

### Fertility Rate & Buyer Pool

The thesis's observation about 1.57 fertility rate and buyer pool thinning is real — but the lag is 25-30 years. The buyer pool for the next decade is already born. What matters more near-term is:
- Immigration (net 1M+ annually, potential policy risk under current admin)
- Household formation vs. marriage/cohabitation trends
- The 105.8M adults not in labor force — this is a demand destruction signal, not a supply signal

### the thesis's Ted Benna / 401(k) Observation

This is the subtlest point in his analysis and the one most people will miss. The 401(k) was designed as a supplemental savings vehicle, not a retirement system. But it became the *de facto* retirement system for America. Similarly, homeownership was designed as shelter, not a wealth-building vehicle. But it became the *de facto* wealth-building mechanism for the American middle class.

The implication: **Nobody has figured out how to build the buyer** who can afford $440,600 median prices with 2.6% savings rates and 3.2% wage growth against 3.8% inflation. The top of the market (Berkshire's Esplanade buyers, $183K+ household income, 750 FICO, 21% down payment) is fine. The bottom is frozen.

This is *why* build-to-rent is the answer. You don't need to build the buyer — you build the renter. And the ROAD Act just gave you permanent permission to do so at institutional scale.

## Summary: The $100M Allocation

| Strategy | Allocation | Rationale | Timeline |
|----------|-----------|-----------|----------|
| Public homebuilder equity (CCS, LGIH, KBH, MTH) | $35-40M | Below-book entries in structural housing deficit. M&A optionality. | 2-5 years |
| Build-to-rent platform (JV or direct) | $25-30M | ROAD Act permanent BTR exemption = legal monopoly for institutional SFR growth. | 3-7 years |
| Housing supply chain equities | $20-25M | Picks-and-shovels play. Materials + manufactured housing benefit from any volume recovery. | 2-5 years |
| Land banking (Sun Belt entitled lots) | $10-15M | Patient capital. Buy below replacement cost, sell into next cycle. | 5-10 years |

### What You're Betting On

1. **The housing shortage is real** (4.7M units per Zillow) and will take a decade+ to close
2. **The ROAD Act's BTR exemption is permanent** and structurally advantages builders over SFR REITs
3. **Interest rates will eventually normalize** or at least stop rising — any easing unleashes pent-up demand
4. **Consolidation continues** — sub-50 builders are being swept up by Berkshire, Japanese acquirers, and larger publics
5. **Nobody has figured out how to build the buyer** — BTR lets you serve the market without needing one

### What Could Go Wrong

1. **Warsh hikes aggressively** (4.25%+) → mortgage rates hit 7.5%+ → sales freeze extends another 2 years
2. **Recession** → job losses → housing demand drops even with supply deficit
3. **Data center capex crowds out residential construction labor** → builders can't ramp even when demand returns
4. **ROAD Act BTR exemption gets narrowed** in future legislation (15-year sunset provision)
5. **Immigration restriction** → household formation slows → demand thesis weakens
