## Legal Landscape

### AB 712 (Effective Jan 1, 2026)
- Authored by Assembly Member Buffy Wicks
- Provides enhanced remedies when developer successfully sues to enforce a "housing reform law"
- **Key provision:** Courts must impose fines of **$10,000 per housing unit** if the developer prevails after the AG previously warned the city
- For 665 units = **$6.65M in potential fines**
- If the city violates the same statute again in the same housing element cycle, fines multiply by 5x = **$33.25M**
- Also mandates attorney's fees for prevailing developers

### AB 2011
- Streamlines housing in commercial zones
- Requires ministerial (non-discretionary) approval if project meets standards
- Projects under AB 2011 are **exempt from CEQA**
- City has determined 3 times that 80 Willow is ineligible for AB 2011

### Builder's Remedy (SB 330 / HAA)
- Limits city's authority to deny affordable housing when city lacked compliant housing element
- N17's preliminary app was filed before Menlo Park's housing element was approved
- City argues it adopted a compliant element before the formal application was processed

### CEQA
- Environmental review has NOT started
- LSA Associates hired but waiting on developer's deposit
- If AB 2011 applies, project would be CEQA-exempt
- This creates perverse incentive: developer may be stalling CEQA deposit to force AB 2011 streamlined path

## Strategic Analysis

The developer's strategy is a three-pronged squeeze:

1. **AB 2011 claim** — If the project qualifies, it gets ministerial approval with no CEQA review and no public hearings
2. **AG pressure via AB 712** — The AG notice creates a litigation setup where the city faces $6.65M+ in fines if a court later finds it violated housing law
3. **Direct litigation threat** — The Aug 3 notice of intent to sue adds immediate pressure

The city is caught between:
- Approving a project its own consistency reviews found inconsistent 5 times
- Facing potentially devastating fines under the new AB 712 framework
- Demolishing a National Register-eligible historic site
- Allowing 458-foot towers in a neighborhood of 1-2 story homes

The AG's intervention is the key variable. If it was prompted by the developer's lobbying rather than the AG's own enforcement priorities, that fundamentally changes the political calculus.
