### Basis and Public Interest

This request serves a significant public interest in understanding whether the Attorney General's AB 712 notice to the City of Menlo Park was initiated through the AG's own enforcement discretion or was prompted by lobbying, complaint, or other solicitation by the developer, property owner, or their representatives.

The AB 712 notice is the first of its kind in two respects. First, Government Code § 65914.2 took effect on January 1, 2026, and the Menlo Park notice (issued on or about July 29, 2026) is the first known AB 712 notice directed at a city's processing of a specific development application, as distinguished from the Attorney General's prior housing enforcement actions against cities that failed to adopt compliant housing elements (e.g., Huntington Beach, Atwater, Ridgecrest, Woodside). Second, the notice carries extraordinary financial consequences: if the city fails to correct the alleged violation within 60 days and the developer prevails in subsequent litigation, Menlo Park faces mandatory fines of $10,000 per housing unit ($6.65 million for 665 units).

This question has taken on added urgency as of August 2026. On August 16, 2026, the Del Mar City Council voted to request the San Diego County District Attorney and the U.S. Attorney investigate AG Bonta over campaign contributions linked to the Seaside Ridge housing development, where donors tied to that project gave $36,400 to Bonta's reelection before the AG intervened in that city's housing decisions. Menlo Park now joins Del Mar and prior housing element cases in a growing pattern where the AG's intervention on behalf of specific projects raises questions about what prompted state action.

Understanding whether this first-of-its-kind enforcement power was deployed at the request of a developer whose property is owned by Willow Project LLC (controlled by the son of a former Russian Energy Minister and subject to Ukrainian sanctions through October 2032), and whose $72 million purchase prompted a suspicious activity report by Deutsche Bank to FinCEN after the bank's New York office initially rejected the transaction, is essential to evaluating the integrity of California's housing enforcement mechanisms and whether AB 712 can be triggered by developer solicitation rather than independent agency oversight. The public has a right to know whether state enforcement power is being deployed with full awareness of the parties whose financial interests benefit from that enforcement.

### Exemption Anticipation and Privilege Log

I recognize that some responsive records may be subject to the deliberative process privilege (Government Code § 7927.500), attorney-client privilege, or the pending litigation exemption (Government Code § 7927.705). To the extent any records are withheld on these grounds, I request a privilege log identifying each withheld record by date, author, recipient, subject matter, and the specific exemption claimed, as required under the CPRA. If any records are withheld under § 7927.705, please identify the specific litigation to which the exemption is claimed to apply and explain how disclosure would prejudice that litigation, as required by Fairley v. Superior Court (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1414.

I note that communications with external parties (developers, lobbyists, consultants) are generally not protected by the deliberative process privilege, which covers only internal pre-decisional agency deliberations, nor by the common-interest doctrine where the external party has an independent financial interest adverse to the public (see California Sportfishing Protection Alliance v. State Water Resources Control Board (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 1285). Communications with a developer who stands to benefit financially from AG enforcement cannot be withheld as joint defense or common-interest privileged. Factual material that is reasonably segregable from deliberative content must be disclosed per Government Code § 7922.575, and any redactions must be narrowly tailored with justification.

Pursuant to City of San Jose v. Superior Court (2017) 2 Cal.5th 608, this request includes communications on personal devices, personal email accounts, and messaging applications (including Signal, WhatsApp, and text messages) where such communications relate to public business concerning the 80 Willow Road project or the AB 712 notice. Please search personal devices and accounts of custodians likely to have responsive communications, including the Attorney General, Chief Deputy AG, and Housing Strike Force leadership.
