### Records Requested

Pursuant to the California Public Records Act (Government Code § 7920.000 et seq.), I request copies of the following records in the possession of the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD):

1. **All communications** between HCD and the California Attorney General's Office regarding the City of Menlo Park's compliance with state housing laws, from January 1, 2023 to the date of this request. This includes but is not limited to:
 - Communications regarding the 80 Willow Road (Willow Park) project
 - Communications regarding Menlo Park's housing element status
 - Communications regarding the applicability of AB 2011 to the 80 Willow Road project
 - Communications preceding, supporting, or following the AG's July 29, 2026 AB 712 notice to Menlo Park, including any email, memo, or meeting note discussing whether to issue the notice
 - Any request from the AG's Office for HCD's technical input or analysis regarding the 80 Willow Road project's eligibility for AB 2011 streamlined processing, including the date of such request and HCD's internal routing or assignment of the request
 - Any HCD response to such a request, including draft or final technical analysis, internal review comments, and any dissenting or concurring views within HCD
 - Any discussion of the legal distinction between AB 712 enforcement for housing element noncompliance versus enforcement for processing a specific development application, including any HCD caution or qualification about extending AB 712 to single-project disputes
 - Any records indicating whether the AG's Office provided HCD with the developer's legal arguments or project materials before requesting HCD's input, versus asking HCD to conduct an independent review

2. **All records** reflecting HCD's technical analysis or determination of whether the 80 Willow Road project qualifies for AB 2011 streamlined processing, including:
 - Any evaluation of whether the project site's C-1 zoning district qualifies as a "commercial corridor" under AB 2011's site eligibility requirements (Government Code § 65912.120), including HCD's interpretation of "commercial corridor" versus "commercial zone" and whether C-1 retail zoning with a 30-foot height limit was intended to be included
 - Any evaluation of whether the project meets AB 2011's affordability, density, and labor standards, including whether 20 percent lower-income units on a site with 458-foot height is consistent with AB 2011's legislative intent
 - Any evaluation of whether the project's proposed 458-foot maximum height is consistent with AB 2011's intended scope for "commercial corridor" developments, given that AB 2011 was drafted for 2- to 4-story commercial conversions
 - Any HCD opinion or advisory letter provided to the City of Menlo Park or the Attorney General regarding AB 2011 eligibility, including any informal guidance or technical assistance that did not rise to a formal opinion
 - Any HCD analysis of the interaction between AB 2011 streamlined processing and CEQA, including whether AB 2011 CEQA exemptions apply to this project and whether a 665-unit tower on a 1.5-acre site was contemplated by the Legislature when granting CEQA exemptions
 - Any internal HCD discussion of the City's multiple AB 2011 determination letters (dated June 22, 2024; November 7, 2025; December 18, 2025; and January 22, 2026), including any HCD assessment of whether the City's determinations were reasonable or supported by substantial evidence
 - Any HCD records reflecting whether the 80 Willow Road site appears on the City's Housing Element inventory of opportunity sites and, if not, whether AB 2011 can be invoked on a non-inventory site that the City has designated for a different density

3. **All records** reflecting coordination between HCD and the Attorney General's Office regarding housing enforcement actions in San Mateo County, from January 1, 2024 to the date of this request, to understand whether the 80 Willow Road situation is part of a broader county-level enforcement pattern or an isolated action.

4. **All records** reflecting any HCD communication with, or technical assistance provided to, the developer (N17 Development, Willow Park LLC, or their representatives) regarding the 80 Willow Road project's compliance with AB 2011, the Housing Accountability Act, or any other state housing law, from January 1, 2023 to the date of this request.

### Basis and Public Interest

HCD's role as the technical authority on housing law compliance makes its analysis central to evaluating the legal merits of both the developer's claims and the city's defenses. The AG's July 29, 2026 AB 712 notice is the first known use of Government Code § 65914.2 against a city's processing of a specific development application (as opposed to housing element noncompliance), and it is directed at a city that has an HCD-approved housing element.

This matters because HCD's technical determinations carry significant weight with courts reviewing Housing Accountability Act claims. If HCD never independently evaluated whether a C-1 zoned site with a 30-foot height limit qualifies as a commercial corridor under AB 2011, or whether a 458-foot tower was contemplated by the Legislature when granting CEQA exemptions for 2- to 4-story commercial conversions, then the AG's notice may rest entirely on the developer's legal theory without the independent technical vetting that AB 712's enforcement scheme assumes.

As of August 2026, the Del Mar City Council has voted to request criminal investigation of AG Bonta over $36,400 in contributions from Seaside Ridge development interests, alleging pay-to-play housing enforcement. Understanding whether HCD's technical role was exercised independently or influenced by the parties who stand to benefit from AG enforcement is essential to evaluating whether the enforcement pipeline is functioning as intended or can be triggered through developer solicitation. The public has a right to know whether the agency tasked with providing neutral technical expertise is providing it, and whether its input was sought before or after the AG decided to act.

### Exemption Anticipation and Privilege Log

To the extent any records are withheld under the deliberative process privilege (Government Code § 7927.500), inter-agency communication exemptions, attorney-client privilege, or pending litigation (Government Code § 7927.705), I request a privilege log identifying each withheld record by date, author, recipient, subject matter, and the specific exemption claimed, with factual material segregated and disclosed per Government Code § 7922.575. If § 7927.705 is claimed, identify the specific litigation and explain prejudice per Fairley v. Superior Court (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1414.

Communications with external parties (developers, lobbyists, YIMBY organizations, Allen Matkins) who have an independent financial or advocacy interest cannot be withheld as common-interest privileged. Communications on personal devices or personal email accounts that relate to public business are public records under City of San Jose v. Superior Court (2017) 2 Cal.5th 608 and must be searched.
