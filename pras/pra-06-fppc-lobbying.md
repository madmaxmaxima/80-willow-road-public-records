### Basis and Public Interest

California's Political Reform Act requires disclosure of lobbying expenditures and campaign contributions to provide public transparency in government decision-making. Given that the Attorney General has taken the unusual step of issuing an AB 712 notice to Menlo Park regarding a specific development project (an action that could expose the city to mandatory fines of $10,000 per housing unit, $6.65 million for 665 units, the first known AB 712 notice for specific project processing), the public has a right to know whether the developer, property owner, or their representatives engaged in lobbying activity or made campaign contributions that may have influenced the state's enforcement decisions.

This question has taken on added urgency as of August 2026. On August 16, 2026, the Del Mar City Council voted to request the San Diego County District Attorney and the U.S. Attorney investigate AG Bonta over $36,400 in campaign contributions linked to the Seaside Ridge housing development (Brian Guiltinan $18,200 on Seaside Ridge team, Mark Arabo $18,200 who hired Guiltinan), where the AG intervened on behalf of that specific 259-unit project. Menlo Park is now the second California city where AG housing enforcement for a specific project coincides with questions about who prompted state action, establishing a pattern that requires transparency.

The developer behind 80 Willow is N17 Development (Oisin Heneghan, founder June 2023, former SVP at Trammell Crow). The property owner Willow Project LLC is controlled by Vitaly Yusufov, son of Igor Yusufov (former Russian Energy Minister 2001-2004, Gazprom board 2003-2013, Rosneft board, Forbes $850M, Ukrainian sanctions through October 2032, Canada sanctions). The $72 million purchase triggered a Deutsche Bank SAR to FinCEN after NY rejected the transaction. Whether any lobbying disclosures or contributions exist for these parties is essential to evaluating enforcement integrity.
