### Basis and Public Interest

HCD serves as the primary state agency overseeing local housing element compliance and has the authority to refer noncompliant jurisdictions to the Attorney General for enforcement. Understanding whether HCD received complaints from the developer or its representatives, and whether those complaints prompted HCD's referral to the AG (if a referral was made), is essential to evaluating the integrity of the state's housing enforcement pipeline.

The AG's July 29, 2026 AB 712 notice to Menlo Park is a first-of-its-kind enforcement action in two respects. It is the first known use of Government Code § 65914.2 (effective January 1, 2026) against a city's processing of a specific development application, as opposed to the AG's prior enforcement actions focused on housing element compliance failures in cities such as Huntington Beach, Atwater, and Ridgecrest. And it is directed at a city that has an approved housing element, making the enforcement theory more novel than the straightforward noncompliance cases that preceded it.

The distinction matters: the AB 712 notice here concerns a single project whose beneficial owner is subject to Ukrainian sanctions (active through October 2032) and whose $72 million property purchase triggered a Deutsche Bank suspicious activity report to FinCEN.

As of August 2026, statewide data shows the AG's Housing Strike Force has brought enforcement actions against approximately 15 jurisdictions for housing element noncompliance since 2022, but AB 712 notices for specific project processing have not appeared in public records. The only other documented AB 712 action was HCD's referral of Huntington Beach over ADU ordinances, not a specific development approval. This makes the Menlo Park notice an outlier that warrants heightened transparency. When this outlier status is combined with the August 2026 Del Mar criminal referral of AG Bonta over housing enforcement tied to campaign contributions, the pattern suggests that AB 712's new enforcement power requires public scrutiny to verify it is not being used as a developer-initiated litigation tool.

Whether HCD independently assessed the project's AB 2011 eligibility or deferred to the developer's characterization will illuminate whether the enforcement pipeline functions as intended or can be initiated through developer solicitation.

If § 7927.705 is claimed, identify the specific litigation and explain prejudice per Fairley v. Superior Court (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1414.

Communications with external parties (developers, lobbyists, YIMBY organizations) who have an independent financial or advocacy interest cannot be withheld as common-interest privileged. Communications on personal devices or personal email accounts that relate to public business are public records under City of San Jose v. Superior Court (2017) 2 Cal.5th 608 and must be searched.
