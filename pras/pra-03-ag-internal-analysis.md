### Basis and Public Interest

The AG's AB 712 notice carries significant legal and financial consequences for Menlo Park and its residents. This request aims to understand the legal foundation and thoroughness of the AG's analysis, including whether the AG evaluated the property's ownership by a family with documented ties to the Russian energy establishment (Gazprom, Rosneft) and subject to Ukrainian sanctions (Council of the European Union and Ukrainian RNBO designations active through October 2032).

The distinction between the AG's prior housing enforcement actions (which targeted cities that refused to adopt compliant housing elements) and this AB 712 notice (which targets a city with an approved housing element over its processing of a single development application) raises a question of first impression: whether the AG analyzed the legal merits independently or relied primarily on the developer's characterization of the dispute. The public has a right to understand whether state enforcement power is being deployed with full awareness of the parties whose financial interests benefit from that enforcement, particularly where foreign sanctions and FinCEN reporting are at issue.

As of August 2026, the Del Mar City Council has voted to request criminal investigation of AG Bonta over $36,400 in campaign contributions from Seaside Ridge development interests, the first formal criminal referral by a California city against its own AG over housing enforcement. Whether the AG conducted independent ownership due diligence before issuing AB 712 notices is central to evaluating whether enforcement discretion is exercised with adequate awareness of who benefits.
